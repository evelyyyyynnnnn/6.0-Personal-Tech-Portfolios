✔️Here I will introduce the first chapter of learning Japanese, which is kana.

✔️ここでは、日本語学習の最初の章である仮名を紹介します。

Japanese uses three scripts at once: **hiragana** for grammar and native words,
**katakana** for loanwords and emphasis, and **kanji** for meaning. Both kana
sets below cover the same 46 sounds — learn hiragana first, katakana second,
and leave kanji until the two kana are automatic.

## Hiragana (ひらがな) — 46

|  | a | i | u | e | o |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **–** | あ a | い i | う u | え e | お o |
| **k** | か ka | き ki | く ku | け ke | こ ko |
| **s** | さ sa | し **shi** | す su | せ se | そ so |
| **t** | た ta | ち **chi** | つ **tsu** | て te | と to |
| **n** | な na | に ni | ぬ nu | ね ne | の no |
| **h** | は ha | ひ hi | ふ **fu** | へ he | ほ ho |
| **m** | ま ma | み mi | む mu | め me | も mo |
| **y** | や ya | | ゆ yu | | よ yo |
| **r** | ら ra | り ri | る ru | れ re | ろ ro |
| **w** | わ wa | | | | を wo |
| **n** | ん n | | | | |

> ⚠️ The four in bold break the pattern: し is *shi* not *si*, ち is *chi*
> not *ti*, つ is *tsu* not *tu*, ふ is *fu* not *hu*. These are where every
> beginner trips.

| **y** | ヤ ya | | ユ yu | | ヨ yo |

> ⚠️ **シ/ツ and ソ/ン are the katakana that get confused.** The difference is
> stroke direction — シ and ツ differ in whether the strokes come in flat or
> steep, and ソ/ン likewise. Write them, do not just read them.

## Voiced marks (濁点・半濁点)

Adding ゛ voices a consonant, ゜ makes the *p* row:

- か ka → が ga ・ さ sa → ざ za ・ た ta → だ da ・ は ha → ば ba
- は ha → ぱ pa （with ゜）

## Why both

The same word written both ways is the clearest demonstration:
**すし** (hiragana, native) and **スシ** (katakana, stylised) — same sound,
different register. Loanwords are almost always katakana: **コーヒー** kōhī,
coffee.
