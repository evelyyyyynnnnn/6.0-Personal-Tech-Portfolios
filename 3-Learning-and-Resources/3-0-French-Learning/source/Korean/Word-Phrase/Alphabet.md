✔️Here I will introduce the first chapter of learning Korean, which is Hangul.

✔️여기서는 한국어 학습의 첫 번째 장인 한글을 소개합니다.

Hangul was promulgated in 1446 under King Sejong. Unlike an alphabet learned
letter by letter, Hangul is **assembled into syllable blocks** — a consonant,
a vowel, and optionally a final consonant, written together in one square.

## Basic consonants (기본 자음) — 14

| 자음 | 소리 | 자음 | 소리 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ㄱ | g / k | ㅇ | ng / silent |
| ㄴ | n | ㅈ | j |
| ㄷ | d / t | ㅊ | ch |
| ㄹ | r / l | ㅋ | k |
| ㅁ | m | ㅌ | t |
| ㅂ | b / p | ㅍ | p |
| ㅅ | s | ㅎ | h |

> ⚠️ **ㅇ is silent at the start of a syllable** and sounds *ng* at the end.
> 아 is just "a"; 앙 is "ang".

## How a syllable is built (음절 구조)

A block is read **top-left → top-right → bottom**:

- ㅎ + ㅏ = **하** (ha)
- ㅎ + ㅏ + ㄴ = **한** (han)
- ㄱ + ㅡ + ㄹ = **글** (geul)
- together: **한글** — "han-geul", literally "great script"

> 💡 The payoff of Hangul is that it is genuinely learnable in a weekend.
> Unlike kanji or French orthography, what you see is what you say.
